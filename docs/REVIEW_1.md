## MUST FIX

1. **The chase WP model is wrong in the last three balls.** With 5 wickets in hand it gives 4 needed off 1 ball a WP of 0.78, 6 off 1 0.55, 3 off 1 0.86 and 8 off 3 0.70 (check). Actual results: 5 off 1 was won 1 time in 10 (model 0.41), 6 off 1 1 in 7 (0.29), 7 to 8 off 3 27% of 22 (0.46). Mean batting-side ΔWP on the last ball of a chase is −2.45 points over 291 balls, where a calibrated model gives 0. The model is fine from 6 balls out (12 to 15 off 6: 0.37 vs 0.39 actual). Cause: the `min(6r/b, 36)/10` term in `lib_wp.chase_features` has coefficient +0.59 (`wp_model_eval.md`) and explodes as b reaches 1; `E_rem` at 1 ball left is 3.1 runs against 2.0 actual. The b ≤ 12 calibration bar cannot see it (118 to 300 balls per bin). Visible damage: all 15 rows of the top-leverage table in `leverage_summary.md` are "1 ball left, 3 to 4 needed" with WP 0.54 to 0.79, and F2 shows them. **Fix:** for chases with b ≤ 6, compute WP by exact backward induction over the 8 outcomes (frequencies from chase balls with b ≤ 6, existing terminal rules) and blend with the logistic by λ = (7 − b)/6. Replace `E_rem` for b ≤ 3 with the empirical mean. Add acceptance bars to `wp_model_eval.md`: 6 off 1 in 0.05 to 0.15; 4 off 1 below 0.30; 12 off 3 below 0.15; mean ΔWP per ball within ±0.5 points for balls-remaining buckets 1, 2–3, 4–6, 7–12 in each innings.

2. **Impact is not centred within situation, so the league-wide "shift" is model misfit and F6's headline is an artifact.** Mean batter Impact per 100 balls should be 0 in any set of states. It is +4.65 routine and −2.65 pressure in the first-innings powerplay, −8.63 in first-innings overs 1–2 pressure (9,262 balls, about 8 SE), −6.55 in chase overs 16–18 pressure and +4.91 in overs 19–20 (check). `eb_params.csv` prior shift is −0.034 for batters and +0.026 for bowlers, which is what F6's title "Batters lose ground under pressure and bowlers gain" reports. These biases are the size of the player-talent SD (about 6 per 100). The shift expected from role mix alone correlates 0.44 with batters' raw shift: openers get −3 to −5 (Kohli −4.2, Abhishek −4.5), new-ball bowlers +3.3 to +4.0 (Bumrah, Bhuvneshwar, Muzarabani). **Fix:** in 05, subtract the league mean credited Impact of the same innings × over group (1–2, 3–6, 7–10, 11–15, 16–18, 19–20) × pressure flag, per discipline, as D30d already does for runs above expected. Write the cell means to `outputs/impact_centering.csv`. Retitle F6 to what survives.

3. **The rating re-inflates noise by z-scoring each shrunk component by its own SD (open question Q4).** Bowlers: sd(D̂) 0.48 vs sd(P̂) 3.70 (k_D 3,811 in `eb_params.csv`), so a point of shift counts about 3 times a point of level, and consistency m sits on the 60 cap while the data implies about 78 and 99. Batters: Mendis is 28th on level (P̂ −0.1, usual −5.0, routine SR 125) and 10th overall, the "poor in every situation" case CLAUDE.md rules out. The time split gives bowler shift a Spearman of −0.09. **Fix:** standardise each component by the SD of its raw (unshrunk) values across eligible players, so shrinkage decides how much of each component survives; remove the m cap. Report the effective weights and a before/after top 10 in `outputs/rating_scale_change.md`.
